Simulated: 1,000 nanowells per well, control rate 1.0 %, 1,000 plates per point, before any small-sample caution (slide 31)

This slide asks the reverse question: if a molecule truly raises the rate, how often would the test find it? We simulated plates with a control rate of 1.0 percent and a true odds ratio between 1.5 and 3; molecule A’s estimate is 2.4. The vertical axis is the share of simulated plates on which the molecule was detected at p below 0.05; 80 percent is the usual target. Three lines show three amounts of well-to-well scatter. When wells are as alike as this donor’s, with r near zero, four and four wells detect an effect of A’s size almost every time, and even an odds ratio of 1.5 more often than not. When wells scatter as molecule B’s do, with r = 0.008, the same true effect is often missed with four and four wells. The right panel doubles the control wells to eight, which recovers part of that loss. So the number of wells needed depends mainly on how much wells differ, which is not known in advance; the scatter seen in the control wells of earlier plates is the best guide. These rates use the plain p below 0.05 rule; slide 31 explains why that rule is a little generous with few wells.
