Next: step 4’s r is 0.0027, not 0: why, and why it matters (slide 17)

This slide does the arithmetic behind the log-likelihood for every well. Under the fit with the gap, control wells have mean 1.000 percent and A wells 2.400 percent, and r is essentially zero, so each well’s chance is a plain binomial: for control well 1, K = 7, it is C(1000, 7) times 0.01 to the 7th times 0.99 to the 993rd, 0.090, whose log is −2.41. The eight logs add to −20.2. Under the fit without the gap, all eight wells share one mean, 1.700 percent, and r rises to 0.0027 to absorb the spread, so the chance is the beta-binomial with a = 6.35 and b = 367: for the same well it is 0.029, log −3.55. Every well is less probable without the gap, because the control wells now sit below the shared mean and the A wells above it; the logs add to −27.4. Twice the difference, 14.2, is the likelihood ratio, and its square root 3.77 is the z of step 5.
