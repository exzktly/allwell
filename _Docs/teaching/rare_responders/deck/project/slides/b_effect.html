Backup · the size of the effect

The effect is an odds ratio between molecule and control

Out of every 1,000 nanowells in a well

Control: about 10 pass the cutoff (first 120 nanowells drawn)

With A: about 24 pass, that is about 14 more (purple)

The count says nothing about the other 976 nanowells of an A well

The same effect, three ways

Odds ratio: 2.4 (95 % interval 1.7 to 3.6)
Rate: 1.0 % → 2.4 % of nanowells
Extra nanowells per 1,000: about 14 (roughly 7 to 25)
Interval for the extra count: from the odds ratio’s interval, at the control rate of 1.0 %

What it does not say
Not “14 responding T cells”: a nanowell past the cutoff is a nanowell whose cells expanded
Not the size of each expansion: only how many nanowells pass

With about 1 control nanowell in 100 passing by chance, most of the 24 in an A well are still real: 14 of 24, about 58 %

A p-value says how sure one can be that a difference is not chance; this slide says how large it is. In a control well about 10 of every 1,000 nanowells pass the cutoff. In a well given molecule A about 24 do. So A adds about 14 nanowells per 1,000 that expand beyond what untreated wells show. The same effect can be stated three ways: as an odds ratio of 2.4, with a 95 percent interval of 1.7 to 3.6; as a rise in rate from 1.0 to 2.4 percent; or as about 14 extra nanowells per 1,000, roughly 7 to 25 when the odds ratio’s interval is applied to the control rate. Two cautions. A nanowell past the cutoff is a nanowell whose cells expanded strongly, not a count of responding T cells. And the test counts how many nanowells pass, not how far each one went. It also follows that in an A well, where 24 pass and about 10 would pass anyway, a little over half of the passing nanowells are attributable to the molecule.
