Three conditions together justify calling a molecule a source of rare responders. There must be at least three control wells and two treated wells, because with fewer the model cannot estimate how much wells differ, and without that a high well cannot be told apart from chance. There must be at least one nanowell past the cutoff among the wells compared. And the molecule’s q value, after the Benjamini–Hochberg correction across every molecule tested on the donor, must be below 0.05; with 16 wells or fewer in the comparison, the model’s p is too small to trust alone, so the q from relabelling the wells, corrected the same way, must be below 0.05 as well. Relabelling cannot go below one over the number of relabellings, so three control and two treated wells, with ten relabellings, can never give a call, and four and four, with seventy, allow a lone hit among up to three molecules. For planning, five control wells allow a useful self-check: split the control wells into a mock treated group of two and a mock control group of three, run the test, and repeat for every split; since nothing separates them, every call is a false positive, and the share of calls measures the false-positive rate on the experiment’s own wells. Six or more control wells with three treated also let the supporting tests, which get their p-values by shuffling well labels, can reach smaller p-values. The six-donor plate gives each donor four control and four treated wells per molecule: enough for a conclusion, one control well short of the self-check. Groups are defined per donor, so each donor’s molecule wells are compared with that donor’s own control wells, because T-cell responses differ between people, and the correction runs over that donor’s molecules only. The model has no term for the plate, so a donor’s wells are treated as one plate. If no control nanowell passes the cutoff, r cannot be estimated and the p-value overstates the evidence. With four control wells r is estimated only roughly and usually too small, which makes the p-value too small; more control wells give a better estimate.
