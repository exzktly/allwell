Background

A DC–T cell assay asks whether a drug wakes up T cells

1. A protein drug

2. A DC shows its pieces on MHC

3. A matching T-cell receptor docks

4. It divides

In patients: anti-drug antibodies

In a dish. DCs from healthy donors are loaded with the drug and cultured with the same donor’s CD4 T cells. T-cell proliferation or cytokines are read about a week later.

It tracks the clinic. Across donor panels, responses agree with how immunogenic drugs are in patients (10 antibodies, up to 50 donors: Joubert 2016).

The catch: drug-specific T cells are rare, about 0.01 to 0.3 per million CD4 T cells for therapeutic antibodies (Delluc 2011).

Jawa et al., Front Immunol 2020 · Joubert et al., PLoS One 2016 · Siegel et al., Pharmaceutics 2022 · Delluc et al., FASEB J 2011

Why a DC–T cell assay. Protein drugs such as therapeutic antibodies can trigger anti-drug antibodies in patients, which can reduce efficacy or cause adverse effects. That antibody response depends on CD4 T cells: a dendritic cell takes up the drug, processes it and shows its peptides on MHC class II molecules at its surface (drawn as cups, each holding one purple drug peptide), and a CD4 T cell whose receptor matches one of those peptides is activated, divides and helps B cells make antibodies (Jawa et al., Frontiers in Immunology 2020, doi 10.3389/fimmu.2020.01301). DC–T cell co-culture assays reproduce that step in vitro: dendritic cells from healthy donors are loaded with the drug and cultured with autologous CD4 T cells, and T-cell proliferation or cytokine secretion is measured after about a week (Siegel et al., Pharmaceutics 2022, doi 10.3390/pharmaceutics14122672). Responses across donor panels have been found to agree with clinical immunogenicity, for example for ten monoclonal antibodies with up to fifty donors (Joubert et al., PLoS One 2016, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0159328). The difficulty, and the subject of the next slides, is that the T cells that respond are rare: for therapeutic antibodies the pre-existing specific CD4 T-cell repertoire in healthy donors is about 0.01 to 0.3 cells per million (Delluc et al., FASEB Journal 2011, doi 10.1096/fj.10-173872).
