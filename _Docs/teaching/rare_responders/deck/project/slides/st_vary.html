Before asking whether a molecule raised the count K, we need to know how much K moves when nothing is going on, so this slide uses control wells in its top row and slide 9’s molecule-B wells in its bottom row. K is how many of a well’s n = 1,000 nanowells pass the cutoff. Top left: if every nanowell in every well had the same 1 % chance, the counts would still differ, as slide 9’s control wells do, 7, 8, 14 and 11, like tossing a thousand coins: that is a binomial, and its spread is counting noise, a variance of n π (1 − π) = 9.9, SD 3. The tilde means “is drawn from”. Bottom left: real wells are never identical, for biological and technical reasons that act on a whole well at once. Each well receives its own random sample of T cells, and responders are rare, so one well may by chance hold two precursors that recognise the antigen and another none. Each well gets its own batch of dendritic cells, which differ in number, viability and how much antigen they took up and present. And each well has its own position on the plate, with its own temperature, evaporation and gas exchange at the edges, and its own focus and timing in imaging. Everything in one well shares these, so they give each well its own true rate πᵢ, scattered around π by an amount set by r; then counting noise acts on top. The letter p is kept for p-values. That two-step model is the beta-binomial. The bottom row uses slide 9’s molecule-B wells because they show it: three sit near 1 %, one near 6 %, a well-level difference that counting noise, SD 3, cannot produce. On the right, the formula shows the two parts multiplied: counting noise, times the bracket 1 + (n − 1) r, which is 1 for identical wells, about 3 at r = 0.002 and 11 at r = 0.01. The curves show what that does to one untreated well’s count, with the mean held at 10: the peak drops and moves toward zero, and a long tail appears to the right, so a count of 20 or more goes from rare, 0.3 %, to ordinary, 15 %. r is never known in advance; it is fitted from replicate wells, as the following slides show.
