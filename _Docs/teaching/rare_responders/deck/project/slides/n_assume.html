The numbers beside the verdict

Odds ratio: exp(δ), how many times higher the odds of a nanowell passing are
95 % CI: the range of odds ratios the data are compatible with
Per-well p: where one well ranks among the control wells
Per-well p = (1 + number of control wells at or above it) ÷ (control wells + 1)
Each A well: p = 0.2, the lowest possible with 4 control wells
Shuffling tests: two further tests that relabel whole wells (slide 25)

What the test assumes

• Wells are treated as independent (they share one estimated cutoff)
• Nanowells in one well are not: r measures how much they share
• One r serves control and molecule wells alike
• “D behaves like z²” is approximate with 4 + 4 wells (slide 18)
• The shuffling tests check that approximation
• One-sided: only a rise counts
• The percentile (99th) is fixed in advance
• The cutoff itself is estimated from the control wells
• With r fitted at 0, nanowells count as independent: p is least reliable
• All wells are treated as one plate: no plate offset is fitted

This backup slide holds what was left off the main test slides. The table gives all three molecules, each tested against the same four control wells: A, with z = 3.77 and p = 0.00008; C, a third molecule on the plate, with p = 0.041; and B, with p = 0.17. The odds ratio is e to the δ, how many times higher the odds of a nanowell passing the cutoff are with the molecule; its 95 percent interval, from the profile of the likelihood, is the range of odds ratios the data are compatible with. The per-well p is a separate, simpler number reported for each well: one plus the number of control wells at or above it, over the number of control wells plus one. With four control wells it cannot go below 0.2, so it describes a well but cannot carry a verdict. The assumptions: wells are independent of one another; nanowells within a well are not, and r measures how much they share; one r serves control and molecule wells alike; the statement that D behaves like z squared is an approximation with four and four wells, which the shuffling tests check; the test is one-sided; the percentile is fixed in advance while the cutoff itself is estimated from the control wells, which all wells then share; when r is fitted at zero, as for A and C, the test reduces to treating nanowells as independent, which is where its p-value is least reliable; and all wells are treated as one plate, with no plate offset.
