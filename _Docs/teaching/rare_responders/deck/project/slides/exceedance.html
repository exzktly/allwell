Nanowell = one DC + a few T cells, imaged and counted on its own

The exceedance test, first half: from nanowells to one number per well. The cutoff comes from the control replicates, not from any one well. The four control wells stay separate: each is drawn as its own swarm, and wells are never merged into one distribution. Only the cutoff draws on all four: it is the fold change that exactly 1 % of all their nanowells together pass, 40 of 4,000, so roughly one dot in each control swarm lies above it. Each dot stands for ten nanowells. That one cutoff is then carried to every well on the plate. The beeswarm has one column per well, each dot one nanowell, on a shared fold-change axis, and one dashed line crosses all sixteen columns at the same fold change. Every nanowell past it is drawn as an orange dot, and their number is the well’s count, K: control 7, 8, 14 and 11; molecule A 21, 24, 28 and 23; molecule B 9, 10, 64 and 13; molecule C 13, 15, 15 and 14. Each well’s thousand nanowells are drawn ten to a dot on a logarithmic fold-change axis, so the orange tail is drawn to scale: about one percent of the dots in a control well. Every well, control or molecule, is counted against this one pooled cutoff, so the four control counts add up to exactly 40. Molecule-A well 1 is the well ringed on slide 5 and drawn on slide 8. The statistics that follow use only these sixteen counts.
