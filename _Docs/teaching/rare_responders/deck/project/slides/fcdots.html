Each row is one well, read left to right in time, then turned into its dots. Orange: past the cutoff. A t-test of the mean misses them: a few orange dots barely move a mean, so the test counts them instead (next slide).

Well = one condition: one test molecule, or the control

Nanowell = one DC + a few T cells, imaged and counted on its own

One step back out from the single nanowell, to whole wells. Each row is one well: the control well on top, the molecule-A well below. Reading a row from left to right is reading forward in time: the same well at the start of imaging and at the end, with the same nine nanowells in the same places. Most nanowells barely change: they end with about as many T cells as they started with, give or take one, so their fold changes scatter around ×1, from about ×0.5 to ×1.7; in the control well that is true of all of them. In the molecule-A well most nanowells look like the controls, but three fill with T cells and pass the cutoff of the next slides, drawn orange. One of them, outlined in dark blue, is number 417, the one followed on the previous slide, going from 2 T cells to 8. Each row then becomes a lane of dots, one per nanowell at its fold change, end divided by start; stacked by fold change, the lane is a small histogram. On the right, the same dots are turned upright and spread sideways, control and molecule A side by side: that is a beeswarm, the plot the real data are shown in, and the rest of the deck uses beeswarms only. The nine molecule-A nanowells were chosen to include two fast growers; in a real well they are a few percent at most, and a real well has about a thousand nanowells.
