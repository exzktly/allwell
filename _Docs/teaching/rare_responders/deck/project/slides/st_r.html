This backup slide supports slides 12 and 23. The fit estimates the gap and r together; r sets how far chance can move the gap. r cannot be looked up; it is estimated from how much replicate wells of the same condition disagree beyond counting noise. Replicates are compared only with replicates of their own condition: the control wells with each other around the control rate, and each molecule’s wells with each other around that molecule’s rate. The model uses one r for every condition, and on a plate like this one the wells of each group carry the information about it: r is fitted from how far wells sit from their own group’s mean. A molecule well is not compared with a control well to estimate r in the fit with a gap; that comparison is the gap itself (slide 17). On the left are this donor’s four control wells, with K = 7, 8, 14 and 11. Their spread, a variance of 10.0, is about what counting noise alone gives, 9.9, so these wells show almost no extra spread and the estimate of r is essentially zero. That does not prove the wells are identical. On the right are three simulated sets of four wells, all drawn from wells that really do differ, with a true r of 0.01. The first set happens to look tight and estimates 0.001, the second 0.005, the third 0.016. Over many such sets the estimate is usually too small: half the time it is below half the truth. An r that is too small makes untreated wells look more alike than they are, so a test built on it is a little too quick to call a difference. More control wells give a better estimate.
