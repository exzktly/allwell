So far: the unknowns are α, δ and r, and any guess of them gives each group a mean rate π (slide 13)
Next: find the guess with the highest log L: maximum likelihood (slide 15)

The model has three unknown numbers, α, δ and r. To find them we need a way to say how good any particular guess is, and that is the likelihood. Panel 1 takes one well and one guess. If the control rate were 1.0 percent and r were zero, the chance of each possible count for a well of 1,000 nanowells is the binomial curve drawn here. Control well 1 actually had K = 7; the height of the curve at 7 is 0.090. That height is written P of K given π and r, each for well i: the chance of exactly that count if the guess were true. Panel 2 does the same for all eight wells under a good guess, 1.0 percent for control and 2.4 percent for A. A likelihood is the chance of the data we actually saw, computed under a guess; the count stays fixed and the guess changes. The dashed curve in panel 1 is a second guess, a rate of 2.4 percent: under it a count of 7 has a chance of only about 0.00003, so that guess scores far worse for this well. A likelihood is therefore a score for a guess, not a property of the nanowells, and it is a different thing from the log-odds of slide 13. Each well is a dot on its group’s curve. The wells are independent, so the chance of all eight counts together is the product of the eight heights; that product is the likelihood of the guess. It is a tiny number, around ten to the minus nine, so we take logarithms, which turns the product into a sum in which each well’s share is visible. That sum is the log-likelihood, log L, here −20.2. Panel 3 scores a different guess: no gap, one rate of 1.7 percent for all eight wells. One curve now has to serve both groups; the control wells sit low on its left flank and the A wells low on its right, and log L falls to -32.3. So log L is a score: the higher it is, the nearer to zero, the better the guess explains the eight counts. The no-gap guess is not just an illustration: it comes back as step 4 of the algorithm.
