So B’s verdict depends on this assumption: a reason to repeat B with more wells, not to call it either way (slide 32)

The test assumes one r for control and molecule wells. Molecule B strains that assumption: its wells scatter far more than the control wells do. This slide shows what happens if each group is given its own r. For A the result stays clearly significant but is less extreme, z = 2.99 instead of 3.77, because the fit without a gap can now give each group its own scatter. C hardly changes, 1.74 against 1.71. B changes most: p moves from 0.17 to 0.029. With its own r, B’s scatter no longer widens the band around the control wells, so the control wells pin their mean down tightly and B’s higher mean stands out more. Why not use this version? Because a scatter estimated from four wells is very rough, and estimating two of them makes the p-value less reliable still; the shared r is the cautious choice when wells are few. The practical conclusion is that B’s verdict depends on an assumption that cannot be checked with four wells. That is a reason to repeat B with more wells, not a reason to call it either way.
