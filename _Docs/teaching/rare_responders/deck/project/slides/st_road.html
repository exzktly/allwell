This slide is the map for the rest of the statistics. Step one: the control wells set one cutoff, the 99th percentile of their nanowells’ fold change, and the same line is used for every well. Step two: each well becomes one number, K, the count of its nanowells past that line. Step three: the beta-binomial model is fitted by maximum likelihood to all eight wells of the comparison at once. It estimates three things together: the control rate, the gap δ between molecule and control, and one shared r, how much replicate wells differ. r is not estimated first and on its own; it comes out of the same fit. For molecule A the gap is 0.89 on the log-odds scale, an odds ratio of 2.4, and r is close to zero. Step four: the same model is fitted again with the gap forced to zero, one rate for all eight wells; r is refitted and now has to absorb whatever gap there was, so it comes out larger, 0.0027 for A. Step five: the two fits are compared. Twice the difference in their log-likelihoods, 14.2, is the likelihood ratio; its square root, 3.77, is a z-score, and the one-sided p-value is 0.00008. Step six: with several molecules on the donor, the p-values are corrected together by Benjamini–Hochberg, giving q, 0.00024 for A. The next slides take these steps one at a time.
